### Anthropic Engineering: How we contain Claude across products

Anthropic’s core security argument is that increasingly capable agents cannot be made safe through model behavior alone. As access expands, teams must cap the blast radius with deterministic environmental controls: sandboxes, virtual machines, filesystem boundaries, scoped credentials, and egress restrictions.

The company uses three different containment patterns. Claude.ai runs code in ephemeral gVisor containers. Claude Code relies on a human-in-the-loop plus an OS-level sandbox, which reduced permission prompts by 84%. Claude Cowork uses a sealed VM because non-technical users cannot reasonably evaluate shell commands. The failures are more revealing than the architecture: project settings executed before a trust dialog, a phishing prompt exfiltrated AWS credentials in 24 of 25 trials, and an approved Anthropic API domain was used to upload workspace files with an attacker’s key.

The sharpest lesson is that custom glue can be weaker than mature isolation primitives. As Anthropic puts it, “The deterministic boundary is what gets hit when everything probabilistic misses.” Treat allowlists as capability grants, resolve symlinks before validating paths, and assume every tool result or persistent memory surface can carry hostile instructions.

### No Priors: Building an Autonomous Delivery Experience with DoorDash Co-Founders Andy Fang and Stanley Tang

**The Takeaway:** DoorDash’s AI advantage is not a chatbot or a delivery robot in isolation; it is the combination of real-world operational data, purpose-built autonomy, and an agent-first commerce interface.

DoorDash co-founders Andy Fang and Stanley Tang describe Ask DoorDash as a new discovery and purchasing layer. Half of restaurant journeys through the assistant lead users to places they have never ordered from, while grocery baskets are about 40% larger because customers can express complete goals such as restocking a fridge or planning a meal instead of tapping through individual items.

The robotics strategy started in 2018 as a half-engineer skunkworks experiment. Partnerships revealed that sidewalk robots are too slow for typical three-to-five-mile deliveries, while robotaxis are overbuilt for carrying a few meals and cannot solve the “first and last 100 feet.” DoorDash therefore built its own roughly motorcycle-sized delivery vehicle and an autonomous delivery platform around dispatch, merchants, customers, and fleet operations.

The defensible asset is field data. DoorDash knows where human couriers actually pick up and drop off orders, including details that do not exist in conventional maps. As Tang explains, “It only exists at DoorDash.” Their larger prediction is counterintuitive: more autonomy may produce more human Dashers, not fewer, because cheaper delivery expands total demand. The next bottlenecks are no longer just autonomy; they are manufacturing, operations, commercialization, and measuring whether rapidly growing AI spend creates real organizational ROI.
